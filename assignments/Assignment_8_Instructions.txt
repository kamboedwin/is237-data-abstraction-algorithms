IS 237 — Assignment 8
Graphs and Graph Algorithms

Purpose
Demonstrate that you can represent graphs, trace BFS/DFS and reason about shortest paths and graph-algorithm complexity.

Part A — Graph representation
Use the undirected graph:
A-B, A-C, B-D, B-E, C-F, E-G, F-G

1. Draw the graph.
2. Write its adjacency list.
3. Write its adjacency matrix.
4. Give the degree of every vertex.
5. State whether the graph is connected.
6. Identify at least one cycle.
7. Compare the storage implications of the two representations.

Part B — Breadth-First Search
Starting at A:
1. Trace BFS step by step.
2. Show the queue after each major step.
3. Show the visited set.
4. Give the final visitation order.
5. Record predecessor and distance values.
6. Reconstruct the shortest unweighted path from A to G.
7. Give BFS time complexity for adjacency-list and matrix representations.

Part C — Depth-First Search
Starting at A and processing neighbours alphabetically:
1. Trace recursive DFS.
2. Draw the call stack at one deep point.
3. Give the final visitation order.
4. Trace an iterative stack-based DFS.
5. Explain why its exact order may differ if neighbour push order changes.
6. Compare DFS with BFS.

Part D — Weighted shortest path
Use this weighted undirected graph:
A-B:4
A-C:2
B-C:1
B-D:5
C-D:8
C-E:10
D-E:2
D-F:6
E-F:3

1. Trace Dijkstra's algorithm from A.
2. Show tentative distances after each settled vertex.
3. Show predecessor updates.
4. Give the final shortest distance to every vertex.
5. Reconstruct the shortest path from A to F.
6. Explain where a min-priority queue/heap is used.
7. State why negative edge weights are a problem for Dijkstra.

Part E — Application decision
For THREE realistic applications:
1. define what vertices and edges represent;
2. state whether the graph is directed/undirected and weighted/unweighted;
3. choose adjacency matrix or adjacency list;
4. choose BFS, DFS or Dijkstra where appropriate;
5. justify the choice using correctness and complexity.
